About this initiative
Ten days of prophetic presence.

The Church has always understood the days between Ascension and Pentecost as a time of waiting — but not passive waiting. We wait with our hands open and our eyes on our neighbors.

Pentecost Action is not a campaign. It is a posture. It is the Church saying: in this moment, we will not look away. We will pray. We will show up. We will speak.

From May 15 to May 24, the Faithful Witness Campaign is inviting congregations to observe these 10 days together — through prayer gatherings, court accompaniment, public witness, and a national mobilization event on Pentecost Sunday.

How to participate
There is a way in for every congregation.

Whether your congregation is just beginning to engage or has been organizing for years, there is a way to participate in Pentecost Action.

1

Pray

Use the Pentecost Action prayer guide in your Sunday service, small groups, or personal devotions during May 15–24. Pray for immigrant families, for your congregation's courage, and for your community.

2

Gather

Host or attend a prayer gathering or vigil in your city during the 10 days. Find one near you on our events page — or register your own gathering on the network map.

3

Accompany

Sign up to accompany an immigrant family to an immigration hearing during Pentecost Action week. No legal training required — just your presence and your willingness to show up.

4

Speak

Contact your representative during Pentecost Action week. Add your name to the open letter. Use our letter-to-the-editor template to share your voice in your local paper.

5

Mobilize on Pentecost Sunday

On May 24 — Pentecost Sunday — join public witness actions happening in cities across the country. Find a mobilization event near you or organize one in your community.

Know Your Rights: A Congregation Guide

12 min read· English & Spanish· Updated March 2025

Essential legal information for churches walking alongside immigrant neighbors — covering rights during enforcement encounters, how to support detained individuals, and how congregations can be a source of safety and accurate information. Written for non-lawyers.

Prefer to print or share? Download this resource as a formatted PDF — available in English and Spanish.

Download PDF ↓
Contents

What are your rights?

Every person in the United States — regardless of immigration status — has constitutional rights. These include the right to remain silent, the right to refuse entry without a judicial warrant, and the right to speak with an attorney.

Understanding these rights is not about resisting law enforcement. It is about knowing the law so you and your neighbors can respond calmly, wisely, and without panic.

Key principle: Rights belong to people, not to their status. Every person made in the image of God has dignity, and that dignity is recognized in the law — even when our political moment suggests otherwise.

If ICE comes to your door

You have the right to remain silent. You do not have to open the door unless officers present a judicial warrant signed by a judge.

  • Ask to see the warrant through the window or under the door before opening
  • An ICE administrative warrant (Form I-200 or I-205) does not give officers the right to enter your home
  • A judicial warrant, signed by a judge, does — know the difference
  • You can say: "I am exercising my right to remain silent and do not consent to a search."
[Infographic — "Judicial Warrant vs. Administrative Warrant: Know the Difference"]

If someone in your congregation is detained

The Church does not abandon its members at the gate of a detention center. Here is how your congregation can respond when someone is detained:

  • Contact an immigration attorney or legal aid organization immediately
  • Accompany the family — do not let them navigate this alone
  • Document everything: time, date, what was said, badge numbers if visible
  • Alert your local organizing group or Faithful Witness contact

How your congregation can help

Your church does not need to be a legal sanctuary to be a place of safety. Churches can be centers of accurate, calm, and reliable information.

  • Host a Know Your Rights training with a legal partner — see our Initiatives page
  • Keep a family preparedness card in your church bulletin
  • Train small group leaders to answer basic questions with confidence
  • Connect affected families with legal resources before a crisis happens

Be a Witness in the Public Square

The Church has a voice in this moment. We are nonpartisan but not neutral — the Gospel makes moral demands, and our elected leaders need to hear from people of faith.

Our policy commitments

Human dignity for all people

Every person is made in the image of God — imago Dei — regardless of citizenship status. Immigration policy must recognize this dignity, not strip it.

Due process and the rule of law

We support a fair legal process for all people, including access to legal representation, impartial hearings, and protection from arbitrary detention.

Humanitarian protections

We call on Congress and the Administration to uphold protections for asylum seekers, refugees, and those fleeing violence — consistent with U.S. law and our international obligations.

Family unity

Policies that deliberately separate families are contrary to the biblical vision of family and community. We oppose them.

Nonviolence in enforcement

We oppose the use of terror, intimidation, or disproportionate force as tools of immigration enforcement. Our neighbors deserve safety, not fear.

About

Who we are and why we're here.

The Faithful Witness Campaign is a shared evangelical initiative committed to forming the Church for courageous, nonviolent, Gospel-rooted engagement around immigration at this moment.

Vision

A Church that speaks with one moral voice — rooted in Scripture, shaped by relationship with those most affected, and courageous enough to act when silence would be easier.

Mission

We are a shared evangelical initiative committed to forming the Church for courageous, nonviolent, Gospel-rooted engagement around immigration at this moment. Inspired by the witness of the early church, we seek to discern and move together to be faithful witnesses to what we have seen and heard.

What makes us distinct

Formation-first

Action without spiritual formation burns out. We prioritize the interior life of the Church because we believe faithful witness is formed before it is enacted.

Nonpartisan but not neutral

We are not a political campaign. We are not aligned with any party. But the Gospel makes moral demands, and we will not pretend otherwise.

Centered on those most affected

Our strategies are shaped by immigrant families, not just by advocates. We listen before we act, and we follow the leadership of those with the most at stake.

Equipping the local church

Transformation starts in congregations. We exist to resource and support local churches, not to replace them or speak over them.

The Church as sanctuary

Sacred spaces must remain places of refuge. We believe congregations can be centers of safety, accurate information, and pastoral care — regardless of their legal sanctuary status.

Impact Story Court Accompaniment Chicago, IL

"We didn't know what to do. Then someone from our church showed up."

FW
As told to Faithful Witness
March 2025 · 6 min read

The following is a story shared with the Faithful Witness Campaign with permission. Names have been changed to protect privacy.

Marisol had been in the United States for eleven years. Her three children — ages 7, 10, and 14 — were all born here. Her husband, Eduardo, had a work visa. She did not. When ICE came to their apartment on a Tuesday morning in January, she didn't know she had the right not to open the door.

She opened it.

Eduardo was detained. By 9 a.m., Marisol was sitting on the floor of her kitchen, her youngest child in her lap, her phone in her hand, and no idea who to call.

"I called the church office," she said. "Not because I thought they could do something. I just didn't know who else to call."

The call that changed everything

The pastor who answered that morning was new — he'd been at Lakeview Community Church for less than a year. He had attended one Faithful Witness training on court accompaniment in October. He had never accompanied anyone to a hearing.

"I told her I was coming," he said. "I didn't know what I was going to do when I got there. But I knew I wasn't going to let her sit on that floor alone."

When he arrived, he found Marisol and her three children, her neighbor, and two other women from the congregation who had heard the news and come on their own. Together, they contacted an immigration attorney through the NaLEC legal referral network. Together, they drove to the processing center.

"We sat in the parking lot for four hours," the pastor said. "We prayed. We called people. We waited. That is what accompaniment looks like — it's not dramatic. It's just not leaving."

Eduardo came home

Eduardo was released on bond three days later, with a hearing date scheduled for April. The congregation has since organized a court accompaniment team — five people who are prepared to go with Eduardo to every hearing until his case is resolved.

Marisol said the hardest part wasn't the fear. It was the sense of invisibility.

"When someone shows up — just shows up, with no agenda, just to be with you — you remember that you exist. That your family exists. That the Church sees you."

The Faithful Witness Campaign trains congregations for court accompaniment — because presence is witness. If your congregation wants to be equipped, visit our Initiatives page to learn how to get started.

What this congregation learned

In the weeks after, Lakeview Community Church hosted their first Know Your Rights training — 47 people attended, including neighbors who are not members of the congregation. They have since connected with three other families navigating similar circumstances.

"I used to think I needed to know what to say before I showed up," the pastor said. "I don't believe that anymore. You just have to show up."

Press Statement March 15, 2025

Faithful Witness Campaign Statement on Recent Immigration Enforcement Actions

Faithful Witness Campaign
March 15, 2025

For immediate release. Media inquiries: contact the Faithful Witness Campaign communications team via the form on our News & Stories page.

The Faithful Witness Campaign — a shared evangelical initiative of CCDA, NaLEC, World Relief, and Undivided — issues the following statement in response to the escalation of immigration enforcement actions across the United States over the past several weeks.

We are deeply troubled by what we have seen and heard from the communities we serve. In cities across the country, immigrant families — many of them longtime members of evangelical congregations — are living in fear. Children are going to school not knowing if their parents will be home when they return. Pastors are receiving calls from families who have had doors broken down, fathers taken, mothers detained. We cannot be silent.

What we believe

We believe that every person is made in the image of God — imago Dei — and that this dignity does not expire at a border. We believe in due process, not because it is politically convenient, but because the biblical tradition has always held that the powerful may not arbitrarily deprive the vulnerable of liberty. We believe in family unity, because the destruction of families is not a policy — it is a wound.

We are nonpartisan. We are not aligned with any political party. We have called on administrations of both parties to uphold these values. We do so again now.

What we are calling for

We call on the Administration to:

  • Immediately cease enforcement actions that separate parents from children
  • Ensure that all detained individuals have access to legal counsel before any proceeding
  • Respect the due process rights guaranteed to all persons under the U.S. Constitution, regardless of immigration status
  • Restore humanitarian protections for asylum seekers and those fleeing violence
  • Ensure that places of worship, schools, and hospitals are not sites of immigration enforcement

We call on Congress to act — with bipartisan urgency — on comprehensive immigration reform that upholds human dignity, due process, and the rule of law. The Church has waited long enough for Congress to do its work.

We call on evangelical pastors and church leaders across the country to speak from their pulpits, to accompany their neighbors to court, to host Know Your Rights trainings in their fellowship halls, and to refuse the comfort of silence when their brothers and sisters in Christ are suffering.

What the Church can do right now

The Faithful Witness Campaign exists to equip and resource the Church for exactly this moment. We have resources for court accompaniment, pastoral care, Know Your Rights training, and policy advocacy — all free, all available now.

Visit our Resource Hub for Know Your Rights guides, court accompaniment checklists, and pastoral care manuals. Visit our Events page for trainings and gatherings in your area. Visit our Join Us page to add your name and contact your representative.

We do not know how long this season will last. We know that the Church has outlasted every empire that has tried to silence it. We know that Acts 4:20 was not written for a comfortable moment. And we know that we cannot help speaking about what we have seen and heard.

Issued jointly by the leadership of CCDA, NaLEC, World Relief, and Undivided on behalf of the Faithful Witness Campaign.
